A story behind the stories

Behind every story,
there's a story of its own.

I've always been interested in the moments that make us who we are: where we come from, what we remember, what we leave unsaid, and the ways we find our way back to ourselves.

My Story

A life shaped by stories

I'm Elena A. Whitmore, a British-Nigerian novelist drawn to the emotional landscapes of everyday life. I grew up between stories told around family tables, half-remembered places, and the questions that seemed to follow me from one chapter of life to the next. Over time, those observations became the beginnings of fiction.

I write contemporary and literary fiction about family, identity, love, memory, and belonging. I'm interested in the complicated ways people care for one another, the histories that travel through generations, and the possibility of making peace with the parts of ourselves we once struggled to understand.

For me, writing is a way of paying attention. It allows me to sit with a question, follow a character into unfamiliar territory, and discover what might be waiting on the other side of a difficult truth.

The beginning

Before I thought of myself as a writer, I was a collector of details: the rhythm of a familiar voice, the atmosphere of a room after an important conversation, the way a place can feel different when you return to it years later. I began writing these observations down, first in notebooks and then in stories.

The stories grew as I did. They became a way to explore the questions I could not always answer in ordinary conversation. Eventually, those pages became the foundation for my novels.

My inspiration

My inspiration often comes from the spaces between big events: a journey home, a family photograph, an unexpected kindness, a letter kept in a drawer. I'm drawn to the relationships that shape us and to the small acts of courage that rarely announce themselves.

I'm also inspired by the many ways people understand home. It can be a place, a person, a language, a memory, or something we spend years learning to create for ourselves.

"I am interested in the things people don't always say, but never quite forget."

— Elena A. Whitmore
The Journey

A writer's path, in chapters.

Some moments shape us quietly. These are the chapters that led me to the page.

Before

A collector of details

Before I called myself a writer, I was someone who noticed things. The rhythm of a familiar voice. The atmosphere of a room after an important conversation. The way a place can feel different when you return to it years later.

2018

The first notebook

What began as scattered observations became a habit. I started writing every day—not for anyone else, but because the pages helped me understand the questions I couldn't answer in ordinary conversation.

2021

The first novel

The Things We Leave Behind was published. It was a story about family secrets, forgiveness, and the past we carry—themes that would continue to shape my work.

2023

A map of borrowed light

My second novel explored belonging, reinvention, and the courage to begin somewhere new. It was a story I needed to write as much as my readers needed to read it.

2025

When the rain remembers

My third novel—a deeply personal story about mothers and daughters, memory, and the complicated meaning of home. It is, in many ways, the book I was always working toward.

The Writing Process

A little patience,
a lot of listening.

My writing process usually begins with a question or an image I can't quite leave alone. I make notes, follow the characters, and let the first version be imperfect.

Then comes the slower work: listening for the right voice, finding the shape of the story, and returning to each page until it feels honest.

I like quiet mornings, handwritten notes, well-loved books, and the kind of walk that gives a difficult paragraph room to breathe. Some days the words arrive easily. Other days, the work is simply showing up again.
